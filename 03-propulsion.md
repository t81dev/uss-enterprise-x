# 03 — Propulsion

## Development ladder

The propulsion architecture is intentionally staged.

### Stage A — Chemical propulsion

Use high-performance chemical propulsion for launch, landing, and early flight testing. Reusability and operational cadence matter more than exotic performance.

### Stage B — Nuclear thermal / nuclear electric

Use nuclear systems to increase endurance and reduce dependence on chemical propellant for deep-space work where appropriate.

### Stage C — Fusion propulsion

Treat compact fusion as a major frontier branch. The architecture should reserve mass, volume, and thermal interfaces for fusion without pretending that a practical compact fusion drive currently exists.

### Stage D — Advanced field propulsion

A faster-than-light system is a **speculative research branch**. An Alcubierre-like metric-engineering concept can be modeled only after specifying the actual stress-energy requirements and identifying the unresolved physics.

No design credit is granted merely because a concept has a name.

## Architecture principle

The production vehicle should be useful without FTL. This prevents the entire program from becoming a disguised assumption that warp drive will eventually appear.

## RCS and vector control

Rotation and attitude control should use distributed, fault-tolerant systems rather than relying on one fictional impulse technology.
