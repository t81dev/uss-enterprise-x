# 05 — Hull and Structure

## Primary structural hypothesis

The vehicle begins as an integrated pressure-bearing **monocoque or semi-monocoque hull**, with propulsion and tankage tied directly into the main load path.

A stainless-steel family is an attractive manufacturing baseline because it offers a combination of toughness, thermal capability, weldability, inspectability, and industrial familiarity. Material selection remains an engineering trade study rather than a predetermined conclusion.

## No decorative structure

Any external appendage must justify itself against:

- launch and acceleration loads;
- vibration;
- debris impact;
- thermal cycling;
- fatigue;
- inspection burden;
- repair complexity.

## Radiation

The hull architecture should prioritize **water, propellant, consumables, and other useful mass as shielding** before adding dedicated shielding mass wherever possible.

## Debris

At high relative velocities, microscopic debris becomes a serious structural threat. The vehicle therefore needs layered protection, compartmentalization, and a survivable damaged state.

The precise hypervelocity threat envelope remains a core quantitative design problem.
